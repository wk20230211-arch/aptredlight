Red Light Therapy OEM Packaging Approval: A Brand Buyer Checklist

Published 20 November 2026 · APTREDLIGHT sourcing desk

Red light therapy OEM packaging approval should connect the brand artwork to the exact product, accessories and packing arrangement being ordered. A correct logo file alone does not show that the carton, manual and contents all match. For brand buyers in the US, UK, Europe and Australia, a shared approval record helps turn separate design decisions into an identifiable production version.

This is a purchasing checklist, not a claim that a particular packaging design has passed testing or that a shipment is ready. Confirm each requirement, available record and responsibility with the supplier for your selected configuration.

1. Identify the product and packaging as one approved set

Start with the quoted model, product revision, destination country and order reference. Give the packaging its own revision identifier. List the retail box, protective inserts, accessory packs, manual, labels and outer carton separately so an approved product cannot silently be paired with an older packaging version.

For a panel with a stand, specify whether the stand travels in a separate carton and how the two packages are identified. For a flexible mat or bag, ask the supplier to confirm the packing arrangement appropriate to its construction and instructions. Do not infer a permitted folding method from a photograph of a different product.

2. Approve artwork before authorizing print production

Provide the editable logo or agreed print file, placement dimensions, orientation and finish requirements. State the reference used to assess color. Review the actual-size proof for text, layout and legibility; an image displayed on a phone is not a controlled color reference.

Record who may approve the artwork and which file revision was accepted. Keep the product logo, startup-screen logo and packaging logo as separate approval items when they are part of the project. Approval of one does not establish approval of the others. Ask for a revised proof whenever the supplier changes the printable area, material, process or layout.

3. Match the contents to a written packing list

Ask for one complete sample pack to be opened against the agreed contents list. Verify the controller, power components, mounting hardware and other accessories by identifier and quantity, not appearance alone. Record the language and revision of the instructions supplied with that configuration.

Destination labeling, warnings and intended claims need review for the actual product and market. Do not print certification marks, medical claims or compliance statements merely because another model uses them. The buyer and supplier should identify the records and qualified review needed before artwork release.

Attach the actual evidence to the record. “Photo received” and “approved” are different states. Give each unresolved item a responsible project contact and a next action. A partially approved matrix should not become an unconditional instruction to print or pack.

5. Separate appearance review from protective-packaging verification

Photographs can help confirm placement and visible contents, but they do not demonstrate resistance to transport damage. Discuss the shipping route, handling assumptions, product mass and proposed verification method before agreeing the protective packaging. If testing is required, request the method, tested configuration, acceptance criteria and resulting report.

Compare the tested pack with the production version, including inserts and accessories. Changes to the carton, cushioning or contents should trigger a review of whether existing evidence still applies. No particular test result or damage rate is implied by this guide.

6. Keep packaging approval separate from shipment release

An approved box is one input to shipment readiness. Before release, request the agreed inspection status, actual packed quantities, carton identifiers and final packing list. Resolve deviations in writing and distinguish goods ready for inspection from goods released for collection. A requested collection date is not confirmation that the carrier has accepted a booking.

For repeat orders, compare the purchase specification with the last approved packaging revision rather than relying on a remembered design. Agree how obsolete artwork and leftover packaging will be handled. Any proposed substitution needs an explicit decision before use.
