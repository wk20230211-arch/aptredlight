What to verify before your first red light therapy OEM sample

A useful red light therapy OEM sample checklist connects your purchase brief to a written acceptance decision. For brand buyers in the US, UK, Europe and Australia, record what was supplied, how it was evaluated and which issues remain open before approving the next project stage.

1. Identify the exact version

Record the model name, wavelength list, LED layout, controller version, accessories and packaging. Ask whether your sample matches the version proposed for bulk production. A shared product name alone does not define a configuration.

2. Make output measurements comparable

Request the measurement distance, meter model, operating mode, measurement positions and test conditions alongside irradiance results. Keep electrical input power, nominal LED ratings and measured optical output in separate fields. Figures collected under different conditions should not be treated as equivalent.

3. Evaluate the experience and documentation

Review controls, mounting, packaging, labels and the instructions supplied with the sample. Request documents that identify the exact model and configuration. Record any unavailable evidence as an open item before your purchasing decision.

4. Put commercial terms in writing

Confirm sample charges, customization scope, minimum quantities, lead time, delivery terms and warranty responsibilities. Agree how specification changes will be approved and how the production order will be inspected.

5. Use one sample acceptance record

Give each physical sample a reference ID and link it to the quotation revision and specification. Agree the evaluation method and acceptance criteria before recording a result. Keep requirements, evidence and decisions in separate columns; a photograph or supplier statement should not silently become a measured result.

Add fields for the responsible person, evidence link, target resolution date and final decision to each row. An unchecked row means not evaluated; it does not mean passed. This worksheet is a proposed buyer process, not a statement that APTREDLIGHT has completed these checks for a particular order.

6. Separate the destination-market brief from sample appearance

For every US, UK, European or Australian launch, name the destination country, intended use, sales channel and proposed marketing claims in the brief. Ask the supplier to identify the quoted electrical configuration, supplied plug or adapter, instruction language and available model-specific documents. Record these as items to confirm, not assumptions based on another buyer's sample.

If one project covers several countries, maintain a separate configuration and document row for each market. A shared enclosure does not show that the power accessories, labels, instructions or evidence are identical. Route any unresolved market-entry or product-claim questions to your responsible compliance reviewer before commercial release; this checklist does not determine regulatory approval.

7. Close sample differences before advancing the order

Use a short issue record: requirement reference → observed difference → evidence → agreed action → owner → recheck result. For example, if the received accessory set differs from the approved packing list, record the missing item, obtain a corrected list or replacement, and verify the resolution. Do not mark the entire sample approved simply because its controls operate.

Ask which changes require a revised sample and which can be checked with updated artwork, documents or other agreed evidence. For changes affecting safety, intended use or measured performance, seek an appropriate technical review rather than accepting a cosmetic photograph. Agree who has authority to close an issue and retain the decision alongside the sample record.

Choose an explicit next-stage decision: accepted for the defined scope; on hold pending named evidence; or revision requested. If an open non-blocking item is accepted for a limited stage, record its scope and restrictions. Approval to continue development is distinct from approval to ship a production order.

8. Hand over the approved reference

When the agreed review is complete, retain the identified reference sample, specification revision, accessory list, artwork, instructions and acceptance record together. Record who holds the sample and how a replacement reference would be approved. Ask the supplier to acknowledge the same version in the production order; the sample alone does not establish the quality of a later batch.

Carry unresolved items and approved changes into the repeat-order change-control record. Use the packaging approval checklist for artwork and packing decisions, and agree after-sales responsibilities separately.
