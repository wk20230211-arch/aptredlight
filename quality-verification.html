APTREDLIGHT / PROJECT VERIFICATION

Quality decisions need
an agreed evidence trail.

Use this five-step project verification framework to define the checks, acceptance criteria and records for the exact configuration you plan to order. It is a proposed buyer-supplier workflow, not proof that every test is completed for every model.

These stages are evidence requests and agreement points. Confirm availability and actual execution records for your project; this framework does not claim an independently certified factory SOP or blanket product approval.

01

Incoming components and configuration

Agree the configuration revision, relevant parts, supply scope and traceability needs. Identify the LED arrangement, power supply, controller, materials and accessories that must match the accepted design.

Evidence to agree and request

Approved configuration, component identifiers where applicable, incoming acceptance requirements and change notification responsibilities.

02

Engineering sample and optical review

Check the actual sample against agreed dimensions, controls, usability and documented optical conditions. State the instrument, measurement plane, distance, operating mode and coverage. Do not equate a photograph or a center reading with complete sample approval.

Evidence to agree and request

Identified reference sample, test conditions, documented results, deviations and signed or otherwise recorded approval.

03

In-process checkpoints

Define the assembly, workmanship and functional checkpoints appropriate to the product and order. Agree defect definitions, inspection methods and responsibility for resolving deviations. There is no universal acceptance threshold supplied by this page.

Evidence to agree and request

Project inspection plan, relevant work checkpoints, defect/deviation records and agreed escalation.

04

Final model and documentation review

Match the finished configuration, labels, instructions, accessories and applicable reports to the order. Review any safety or reliability evaluation needed for the intended use and destination. Do not treat a component certificate as finished-device approval.

Evidence to agree and request

Final inspection scope, model/revision identifiers, document list, label/manual versions and open-item decisions.

05

Packing, shipment and repeat-order traceability

Agree packing, quantities, handling, identification, shipping responsibilities and after-sales arrangements. Preserve the approved configuration for repeat orders and define which changes require buyer review.

Evidence to agree and request

Packing checks, order/batch identification, delivery assumptions, issue-handling terms and repeat-order revision control.

Keep unresolved items visible.

Use a project record to identify the requirement, available evidence, reviewer, outcome and next action. If a report or test does not exist for the exact model, record the gap and the proposed validation instead of describing it as complete.

Separate agreed acceptance from market authorization. A well-documented sample decision does not, on its own, establish permission for a claim or sale in every destination.
